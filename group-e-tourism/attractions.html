Tourist Attractions

Explore the most captivating destinations in Ghana's Central Region — from ancient castles to pristine beaches and lush rainforests.

The Central Region boasts an extraordinary array of attractions — UNESCO World Heritage Sites, national parks, sacred shrines, serene beaches, and unique eco-lodges. Below are our top picks to make the most of your visit.

Discover Our Top Attractions

Eight must-visit destinations in Ghana's Central Region

UNESCO Heritage

Cape Coast Castle

Standing on the shores of the Gulf of Guinea, Cape Coast Castle is one of the largest slave-holding sites in West Africa. Declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979, it served as the headquarters of British colonial administration and a major embarkation point for enslaved Africans during the transatlantic slave trade. Today, its dungeons, "Door of No Return," and museum offer a profoundly moving experience. Guided tours bring history to life and provide crucial context for understanding the African diaspora.

📍 Cape Coast ★★★★★ 4.9 ⏰ 2–3 hrs
Nature Reserve

Kakum National Park

Covering 375 square kilometres of pristine tropical rainforest, Kakum National Park is one of Ghana's premier nature destinations. Its centrepiece is the famous canopy walkway — Africa's first — consisting of seven bridges suspended 30 metres above the forest floor, stretching 330 metres through the emerald canopy. Home to forest elephants, leopards, over 400 bird species, and thousands of plant varieties, it is a paradise for nature lovers, birdwatchers, and adventure seekers. Night walks and forest treks are also available.

📍 Kakum, Cape Coast ★★★★★ 4.8 ⏰ Half-day
UNESCO Heritage

Elmina Castle (St. George's Castle)

Built by the Portuguese in 1482, Elmina Castle is the oldest European structure in sub-Saharan Africa and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Originally constructed for trade in gold and ivory, it was later expanded by the Dutch and converted into a major slave-trading post. The castle's dungeons, torture chambers, the infamous "Door of No Return," and the chapel above the female dungeon together tell a harrowing story of exploitation. The picturesque town of Elmina surrounding the castle is equally charming, with colourful fishing boats and a lively market.

📍 Elmina ★★★★★ 4.9 ⏰ 2–3 hrs
Historical Site

Assin Manso (Slave River)

Assin Manso holds enormous significance in the history of the transatlantic slave trade. This was one of the last stops where enslaved Africans were bathed in the Donkor River before being marched to the coast for embarkation. The site now features the Ancestral Slave River, a memorial cemetery where the remains of two enslaved Africans — one from Jamaica and one from the United States — have been repatriated and buried. Every year, thousands of members of the African diaspora make emotional pilgrimages to this sacred site to reconnect with their roots.

📍 Assin Manso ★★★★☆ 4.6 ⏰ 1–2 hrs
Eco-Tourism

Hans Cottage Botel

One of Ghana's most unique and charming eco-lodges, Hans Cottage Botel sits on a serene lagoon teeming with crocodiles — yet remarkably, the crocodiles here are believed to be sacred and have never harmed a human. Visitors can walk among these ancient reptiles, touch them, and even take photographs. The stilted wooden cottages and restaurant over the water make for a magical dining experience. Located just 8km from Cape Coast on the Kakum road, it is the perfect stop between the city and the national park.

📍 Cape Coast Road ★★★★☆ 4.5 ⏰ 1–3 hrs
Beach

Brenu Beach

Brenu Beach is one of the most beautiful and serene stretches of coastline in the Central Region. Located a short drive west of Cape Coast, this pristine beach is flanked by swaying palm trees and lapped by warm Atlantic waters. It is perfect for swimming, sunbathing, and beach volleyball. The beach is famously quieter than some other spots, making it ideal for a peaceful escape. A freshwater lagoon sits just behind the beach, creating a unique dual-water environment that delights nature lovers. Several beach resorts and restaurants line the shore.

📍 Brenu-Akyinim ★★★★☆ 4.4 ⏰ Half-day
Beach & History

Anomabo Beach & Fort

Anomabo is a historic coastal town east of Cape Coast that combines beautiful Atlantic beaches with significant historical heritage. The town's beach is wide, clean, and lined with colourful fishing canoes that paint a vivid tableau of daily Ghanaian life. Fort William, a British-built slave fort now converted into a heritage museum, overlooks the sea and tells the story of Anomabo's role in the slave trade. The town is also renowned for its vibrant fishing community and the famous "Anomabo" brand of traditional canoe-building. Fresh fish is sold daily right on the beach.

📍 Anomabo ★★★★☆ 4.5 ⏰ Half-day
Culture & Tradition

Asafo Posuban Shrines

The Posuban Shrines of Elmina and Mankessim are among the most fascinating and distinctive cultural monuments in Ghana. Erected by the Asafo — traditional military companies of the Fante people — these colourful concrete and cement structures serve as regimental headquarters and are adorned with vivid figurines, biblical scenes, mythological creatures, and military symbols. Each company's shrine is unique, reflecting its own history, totems, and identity. There are 77 known Posuban in the Central Region, concentrated in towns like Elmina, Cape Coast, Anomabo, and Mankessim. They are living cultural symbols still actively maintained and used.

📍 Elmina / Mankessim ★★★★★ 4.7 ⏰ 1–2 hrs
Royal Heritage

Eguafo Royal Palace

The ancient palace of the Eguafo royal family offers a rare glimpse into traditional Ghanaian royalty. Located near Elmina, the palace dates back to the 17th century and features an original Dutch-influenced architecture blended with Fante traditions. The current chief and his family still reside here, and visitors are welcomed into rooms filled with centuries-old artefacts, royal regalia, and stories passed down through generations. The surrounding village of Eguafo retains much of its traditional character with narrow streets and colonial-era buildings.

📍 Eguafo, near Elmina ★★★★☆ 4.3 ⏰ 1 hr
Beach

Abanze Beach & Lagoon

Abanze is a hidden gem along the Central Region coastline, featuring a stunning beach where a freshwater lagoon meets the Atlantic Ocean. The area is largely undeveloped, making it a pristine escape for those seeking solitude and nature. Sea turtles nest on the beach seasonally, and community-based eco-tourism initiatives allow visitors to participate in turtle conservation programmes. The surrounding area also offers mangrove canoe tours and traditional fishing experiences with local communities.

📍 Abanze, Central Coast ★★★★☆ 4.4 ⏰ Half-day
