Travel Tips

Everything you need to know to plan a safe, enjoyable, and memorable trip to Ghana's Central Region.

Whether you are a first-time visitor or returning traveller, these tips will help you make the most of your time in the Central Region. Our tourism team has compiled practical advice to ensure a smooth and enriching experience.

Best Time to Visit

Ghana's Central Region can be visited year-round, but seasons vary significantly

Month Season Weather Tourism Activity Best For
November – February Dry Season (Harmattan) Warm, dusty winds; 24–32°C Peak Season Sightseeing, beach, festivals
March – May Pre-Rains / Transition Hot & humid; 27–34°C Moderate Nature, birdwatching
June – August Major Rainy Season Wet, green; 23–29°C Off-Peak Budget travel, lush scenery
September – October Minor Dry Season Partly dry; 25–31°C Good Wildlife, canopy walks
🌟

Pro Tip: The Best Months

  • December – February is the most popular period — dry, sunny, and cooler in the evenings. Perfect for castle tours, beach visits, and Kakum walks.
  • May is ideal for the Aboakyer Festival in Winneba — one of Ghana's most spectacular annual cultural events.
  • July – August offers great deals on accommodation and you'll find fewer crowds at the castles.

Getting There & Getting Around

Transport options for reaching and exploring the Central Region

✈

By Air

Fly into Kotoka International Airport in Accra. From there, Cape Coast is approximately 3 hours by road (165km). No domestic flights serve Cape Coast directly.

🚌

By Bus / Coach

VIP Jeoun and STC buses run daily from Accra to Cape Coast. Comfortable, affordable, and recommended. Journey time: 2.5–3.5 hrs depending on traffic.

🚗

By Shared Taxi (Trotro)

Trotros (minibuses) connect Accra to Cape Coast very cheaply. They depart from Accra's Kaneshie station. Great for the budget-conscious but can be crowded.

🚖

By Private Car / Hire

Renting a car or hiring a private driver is the most flexible option. Roads to Cape Coast are paved and in good condition. Drivers can arrange multi-site day tours.

🚲

Within Cape Coast

Shared taxis, motorbike taxis (okada), and tuk-tuks serve the city. For Kakum National Park, hire a taxi or join a guided tour departing from central Cape Coast.

🚑

Tour Operators

Local tour operators in Cape Coast offer excellent day packages covering multiple sites — castles, Kakum, Hans Botel, and beaches. Highly recommended for first-timers.

Essential Travel Tips

Practical advice to help you travel confidently and responsibly

🏠

Accommodation

  • Cape Coast has hotels ranging from budget guesthouses to mid-range resorts. Book 2–3 weeks ahead during peak season (Dec–Feb).
  • Elmina offers charming boutique guesthouses near the castle.
  • Hans Cottage Botel provides unique over-water bungalows.
  • Several eco-lodges operate near Kakum National Park for immersive forest stays.
  • Beach resorts at Brenu and Anomabo offer affordable beachfront accommodation.
💵

Currency & Money

  • The official currency is the Ghanaian Cedi (GHS). Keep some cash handy as many smaller vendors and rural sites don't accept cards.
  • ATMs are available in Cape Coast and Elmina. Barclays, GCB, and Ecobank are reliable.
  • USD, EUR, and GBP can be exchanged at forex bureaus — rates are generally competitive.
  • Mobile money (MTN MoMo, Vodafone Cash) is widely used for local payments.
  • Always carry small denomination cedis for markets and entry fees.
⚡

Safety Tips

  • Ghana is one of West Africa's safest countries, but remain vigilant as in any tourist area.
  • Keep valuables secure; use hotel safes when available.
  • Avoid displaying expensive jewellery or electronics in crowded areas.
  • Ocean swimming carries risk — observe local warnings and swim in designated areas.
  • At night, use pre-booked taxis rather than hailing random vehicles.
  • Keep a copy of your passport and travel documents in a separate bag.
🏥

Health & Medical

  • Malaria is present — take prophylactic medication and use insect repellent and bed nets.
  • Drink bottled or purified water only. Avoid tap water and ice in questionable establishments.
  • Yellow fever vaccination is required for entry into Ghana.
  • Recommended vaccinations: Typhoid, Hepatitis A & B, Tetanus.
  • Cape Coast Government Hospital and several private clinics can handle most medical needs.
  • Carry a basic first-aid kit including rehydration salts and sun protection.
📦

What to Pack

  • Lightweight, breathable cotton clothing for the tropical heat.
  • Comfortable walking shoes for castle tours and forest hikes.
  • Sturdy sandals for beach visits.
  • Rain jacket or poncho if visiting June–October.
  • High-SPF sunscreen — the equatorial sun is intense.
  • Insect repellent (DEET-based) for evenings and forest visits.
  • Power adapters — Ghana uses Type G (British) plugs, 230V.
🌐

Visas & Entry

  • ECOWAS citizens (15 West African nations) can enter Ghana without a visa.
  • Most other nationalities require a visa — apply online via the Ghana Immigration Service e-Visa portal.
  • E-visas are typically processed within 5–7 business days.
  • Valid passport required with at least 6 months remaining.
  • Yellow fever vaccination certificate is mandatory at entry.
📱

Connectivity & Communication

  • Buy a local SIM card on arrival (MTN, Vodafone, or AirtelTigo). Data is cheap and widely available.
  • 4G coverage is good in Cape Coast and Elmina; rural areas may have 3G or 2G only.
  • Wi-Fi is available at most hotels and several restaurants.
  • Ghana country code: +233. Cape Coast area code: 033.
  • WhatsApp is the dominant communication tool — locals use it extensively.
💉

Culture & Etiquette

  • Ghanaians are famously warm and welcoming — greet people with "Akwaaba!" (welcome).
  • Always use your right hand to give or receive items — using the left hand is considered disrespectful.
  • Dress modestly when visiting traditional communities and chief's palaces.
  • Ask permission before photographing individuals — a small tip is appreciated.
  • At historical sites like the castles, maintain respectful silence in solemn areas.
  • Bargaining is acceptable at markets but do so respectfully.
🍲

Food & Dining

  • Must-try local dishes: Fufu with light soup, Banku and tilapia, Kelewele (spicy plantain), Jollof rice, Kontomire stew.
  • Fresh seafood is excellent along the coast — grilled fish and prawns straight from local fishermen.
  • Chop bars (local eateries) offer cheap, authentic Ghanaian meals.
  • Bottled water, Coca-Cola, and Ghanaian malt drinks are widely available.
  • Cape Coast has several restaurants with international and fusion cuisine for varied tastes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common questions from visitors

The journey takes approximately 2.5–3.5 hours. Alternatively, shared trotros (minibuses) are cheaper but less comfortable. Private taxi hire or a rental car gives you the most flexibility.

As with any destination, exercise normal caution: keep valuables secure, avoid poorly lit areas at night, and use reputable transport. The local population is extremely welcoming to visitors.

English is the official language of Ghana and is widely spoken. The predominant local language in the Central Region is Fante (a dialect of Akan). A few basic Fante phrases go a long way: "Akwaaba" (welcome), "Medaase" (thank you), and "Wo ho te sεn?" (How are you?). Most tourism staff, hotel workers, and guides speak good English.

We strongly advise against drinking tap water in Cape Coast or anywhere in Ghana. Stick to bottled mineral water, which is widely available and inexpensive. Sealed sachet water ("pure water") sold by street vendors is also generally safe. When dining out, opt for bottled drinks and avoid ice unless you are certain it was made from purified water.

Yes, several ATMs are available in Cape Coast town — GCB Bank, Barclays (Absa), Stanbic, and Ecobank all have branches with functional ATMs. Visa and Mastercard are accepted. However, smaller restaurants, market vendors, entrance fee kiosks, and rural sites often only accept cash. Always carry a sufficient supply of Ghanaian cedis for day trips outside the city.
